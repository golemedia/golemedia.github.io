From as young as I care to remember we had an authentic pachinko machine that my grandfather brought back from Japan. I would spend tons of time flicking the metal bar and launching the little steel balls around the play area. I loved watching them bounce around all of the pins and into the win pockets. As time went on the balls would slowly disappear — this was decades before Amazon, and replacements were not easy to come by. Eventually we had too few to make the game work and it was retired to a plastic bag in the back of the garage.

Almost 30 years later I came upon it and decided to take a look. It was in surprisingly good shape. Aside from some cobwebs, a bit of dust, and maybe a mouse dropping or two, it was physically and functionally near perfect. There was a lot of corrosion around the ancient 9v battery and connectors, but once cleaned up and replaced even the original lights still worked.

I decided to add this to my list of projects. After some research I confirmed it was an original Sanyo Bussan machine from 1974. I was also able to find manuals, advertisements, and sales brochures from the era — amazingly vague documents, all of them. The export of these machines was highly regulated, and almost all that came to the US went through one or two importers. Even today, parts are near impossible to come by and just about everything needs to be repaired rather than replaced. The emergence of 3D printers has revitalized interest by providing a way to manufacture parts as needed — fortunately that wasn't a problem I ran into.

![Machine interior detail](20250710_225714967_iOS.jpg)

I started disassembling everything to give it a thorough cleaning, lubing, and adjusting anything slightly out of whack. That went well, but a few things remain before reassembly: the chrome frame needs polishing, a couple of small screws need to be sourced, and the plastic cover for the keyhole needs to be re-chromed. Then with a fresh set of balls it will be good to go.

I do plan on reworking the lights and power supply — I'm thinking about replacing them with LEDs and a modern power source to keep it sustainable well into the future. I'm also still deciding whether to leave it as a standalone unit or build a cabinet, and if so whether it should be tabletop or wall mounted.
