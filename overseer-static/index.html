The dot rule. A write fires from that file — not the project, not the index — and travels one hop along every link it has. One hop, because a note's links go stale when it changes; theirs do not. The index has no special status; it is just the node most writes reach.

GET /state — SIMULATED

A demonstration of the spec, not live data. On OSVC-01 these come from its own API and a node pulses while its app runs. MQTT was dropped as the interface 4 Sep — an API serves state, counts and completion in one place and removes a broker from a Pi booting off microSD. Nothing here can be live: measured this session, a Cowork container reaches no RFC1918 address and the device shell has loopback only.

Node = one file. Radius ∝ √(link count), so area tracks the count instead of exaggerating it — and project notes get a further 1.2×, because they are the subject and everything else is context. Colour is membership, not status: each project has its own, everything else in the vault is one dim grey, and all seven code repos share one blue — which repo a file belongs to is the label's job, not the fill's. Two rings: the vault outside, the code repos inside — the same boundary vault-audit-protocol.md draws when it excludes repos/ from every vault metric. Repo membership is git ls-files, which excludes vendored trees for free: EDWinPit-Radio's host/ holds 1,624 files and tracks 12; PANELcore's venv/ holds 575 and tracks 8. Hover any node for its path and link count; click it to fire a write.

Trading — the six object lessons

IBKR · positions, trades, 3-month daily bars, company themes — read live 4 Sep

Bought on a hunch, chosen for their connection to data centres, held with no strategy and no exit. That is the stated purpose — an instrument for learning how this works. It has been unusually generous. Six weeks produced six lessons that are measurable from your own fills rather than borrowed from a book.

Your first counterparty is the fee, and it did not behave consistently

Three of four tickets cost exactly 1.000% of notional. The fourth — same account, same week, same kind of instrument — cost nothing. Nobody should have to guess which. Before any position is sized for real, that discrepancy is a question for IBKR, not a rounding error.

total commission $0.1494 on $19.9086 deployed · 0.750% · 27% of the whole loss at the 3 Sep close, paid before the market moved
2

You own one idea three times

The hunch was accurate — all three genuinely are data-centre exposure. That is the problem. IBKR's own theme graph puts Data Centers as Seagate's first theme and lists Micron under Data Centers, AI and Cloud Computing; IYW's largest holdings are the rank-1 companies in those same themes. Three tickets, one bet. The daily returns agree.

correlation of daily returns, 61 sessions — IYW·MU +0.73 · MU·STX +0.71 · IYW·STX +0.60
3

Equal dollars is not equal risk

Roughly $5 went into each. But Micron moves about three and a half times as much as the ETF does, so the risk split was never even — most of what the account did was Micron and Seagate, with IYW along for the ride. Sizing by dollars feels neutral and isn't. Sizing by volatility is the thing that makes three positions behave like three positions.

annualised volatility — MU 96.6% · STX 83.6% · IYW 28.2%
4

Six weeks measures noise, not skill

Eight days after buying, the account was down 10.5%. Three weeks after that it was up 8.2% and every decision looked vindicated. Today it is down 2.6%. Nothing about the reasoning changed across any of it. The same holding will read as a triumph or a mistake depending on which Tuesday you check, which is precisely why a written thesis and a written exit have to exist before the chart starts talking.

account NAV, time-weighted — $22.37 (29 Jul, −10.5%) → $27.04 (14 Aug, +8.2%) → $24.35 (3 Sep, −2.61%). Opening NAV at inception 27 Apr was $25.00.
5

The better of your two Seagate buys was luck, and that is the useful part

You bought STX twice, six days apart, at 891.03 and then 801.64 — no new information, no changed view. The second fill is effectively flat today; the first is down over ten percent. Same name, same thesis, same week, a ten-point spread in outcome decided by the calendar. Entry timing at this horizon is a coin toss. Cost and size are not — they are the two things fully inside your control, which is where attention belongs.

at the 3 Sep close — STX 21 Jul −10.37% · STX 27 Jul −0.38% · blended −5.57%
6

The journal was wrong about the date, and silent about the exit

The board recorded a single entry on 2026-07-29. There were four fills across two dates, and neither was the 29th — that was the day of the low, which is a plausible way for a wrong date to get remembered. More important than the date: nothing anywhere says what would make you sell. An unstated exit is not a long-term hold, it is an open question that compounds.
